Kafka Topic Partitioning Management
The discussion focused on Kafka's topic management and consumer partitioning. GeeksforGeeks explained that while creating a topic without specifying partitions results in a single partition by default, Kafka allows altering topics to add more partitions as consumer requirements change. The key constraint was explained: within a consumer group, only one consumer can consume from a single partition to avoid data duplication and state management issues. The team discussed how producers must coordinate with consumers to ensure appropriate partition configurations based on production and consumption rates, with the ability to dynamically adjust partitions as consumer groups scale.


Kafka Partitioning and Consumer Groups
The meeting focused on explaining Kafka's partitioning and consumer group concepts. The instructor explained how Kafka partitions data across multiple servers for load balancing and fault tolerance, with a recommended ratio of one partition per consumer to optimize performance. They demonstrated how to modify partition counts and showed examples of consumer groups, explaining that consumers within the same group can read from different partitions while consumers in different groups cannot read the same messages. The instructor also covered Kafka's sticky partitioner implementation, which replaced the previous round-robin approach in version 2.4, and explained how producers can specify keys to control message distribution across partitions. The session concluded with a discussion about message deduplication and idempotency, where the instructor explained that consumers should maintain their own state to prevent duplicate message processing.
